# Edit the document, not just its sentences

Use for multi-paragraph answers, articles, product pages, reports and instructions. Preserve the requested scope: reorganize only when the user asks for a rewrite or when the allowed edit requires it.

## Give each section a job

Identify the reader's question, the main answer and the action or understanding the document should support. Put the answer where it helps the reader; retain prerequisites before dependent instructions. Keep a definition or explanation near its first needed use. Preserve a deliberate narrative or argument when that is the requested form.

Look for the same claim repeated in the introduction, section openings and conclusion. Keep its clearest useful occurrence and consolidate supporting evidence. Similar words can carry different conditions, audiences, dates or exceptions: reconcile those differences before merging. Do not collapse distinct product capabilities into an unsupported broader promise.

Repeated content can be useful at separate decisions, in a standalone summary, or in warnings beside different risky actions. Evaluate each occurrence by its purpose, not by a duplicate-word count. A summary may repeat the outcome without retelling every reason.

## Preserve the connections

After cutting or moving text, check pronouns, transitions, definitions, sequence, comparisons and section references. Keep citations attached to the claims they support. Do not move a price, eligibility condition or unresolved dependency away from the action it qualifies. If the sources conflict, expose the specific conflict rather than selecting the simpler version.

In a substantial rewrite, quietly compare the original and revision for essential claims, actors, conditions, quantities and next steps. This comparison is an editing aid, not a required user-facing table. Protected quotes, code, placeholders, links and source data remain unchanged unless the user authorizes their modification.

## Fit the reader

Cut redundant explanation while retaining the example or reasoning the reader needs. A novice may need a short definition that an expert does not. A support reply may need acknowledgment; removing every courteous phrase can make it needlessly abrupt. Preserve purposeful voice and the reader's technical vocabulary.

When compression would make the task harder, explain or restructure instead. Do not turn an article into a list, flatten a story into a summary, or replace a precise term with a vague familiar word just to reduce length.

## Notes and credits

[Agrawal and Carpuat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10126) use reading comprehension to test retained meaning in context. These motivate checking what the reader can still understand after an edit; they do not establish Mojo's performance.
