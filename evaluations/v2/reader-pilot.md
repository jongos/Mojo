# Reader pilot kit

Prepared protocol; no reader sessions have been conducted.

Recruit intended readers only with authorization and an agreed data-handling plan. Use fictional benchmark content. Keep generator conditions hidden and counterbalance which version each reader sees first. For each task, collect the answer before asking for a preference, so a polished tone does not substitute for comprehension.

Choose short instructions, a longer document, a final handoff and a consequence-sensitive rule. Use the benchmark's comprehension questions and answers. Present only the selected version to a reader for a given case; repeated exposure to the source can teach the answer. Ask the reader to locate the needed information, state the relevant condition in their own words and identify the next action. Record correctness, omitted conditions and requests for clarification. A reader may correctly say that the text does not establish an answer.

For voice, ask whether the text fits the stated audience and situation, and what wording feels out of place. For document structure, ask where the reader looked and what they expected there. Do not ask whether the text sounds AI-generated. Collect optional completion time only under consistent conditions; a small pilot cannot establish population-level speed gains.

Use one record per reader/case: anonymous participant code, case ID, assigned version, questions, verbatim answers with permission, task outcome, observed obstacle, and optional preference with reason. Do not place participant identities or private source text in this repository. Keep the session data in an approved location and publish only authorized aggregate findings.

Review failures, revise the relevant guidance, and test again with fresh cases or readers. Report the pilot size, recruitment, procedure and limits. No human-comprehension or conversion claim should be inferred from the model evaluations in this directory.
