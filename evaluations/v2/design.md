# Mojo writing benchmark v2

Sixty fictional cases span six families: short edits (S), document editing (D), conversation updates (C), voice (V), meaning (M), and structured content (F). Each family contains ten materially different cases. Cases 01–05 are development; 06–10 are heldout. Cases 01–02 in each family form the twelve-case pilot, which is a subset of development rather than an additional split.

The corpus tests when to shorten, when to retain good original writing, and when a useful edit requires more explanation. Four long source documents cover a library relocation, kitchen pilot, park reopening, and museum research service. They distribute repetition and essential distinctions across paragraphs. Other cases cover changed hypotheses, status versus completion, self-contained final answers, source contradictions, voice retention, eligibility logic, permission scope, strict bounds, and immutable structured elements.

`cases.json` contains prompts only. `rubric.json` is keyed by stable case ID and contains semantic invariants, comprehension questions with expected answers, protected exact substrings, and hard failure criteria. Keep rubrics out of generation prompts. No sample rewrites, model outputs, judgments, or existing skill text were used to construct this corpus. `build_corpus.py` is the deterministic corpus source and validates counts, splits, long-document lengths, and protected substrings.

The fixed split is balanced within family, not randomly sampled. Holdout prompts should remain unseen during skill revision; public inclusion in this repository alone cannot enforce that boundary. All material is synthetic and does not establish professional, statistical, or real-user performance. Sixty cases provide directional coverage rather than population estimates. The rubric is necessary but not exhaustive: semantic success does not by itself establish good writing, and brevity alone is not success. Evaluate reader comprehension and task usefulness alongside fidelity. Do not reward changes to already effective originals merely because they are changes.
